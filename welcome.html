What gets built

Two halves, one platform.

A public side that recruits members and carries your krewe's story. A members' side that runs the season. Same site, one login between them.

The side everyone sees

Your story, out front

Open to the public. Built to be found and to bring people in.

  • History and heritage. The founding, the traditions, the costume, the years — the things that make someone want to belong.

The side that runs the season

Behind the member login

Officers get the keys. Members get one place to go.

  • A directory that stays current — members update themselves, so nobody maintains it by hand.
  • Event RSVPs. Know your headcount before parade day, not on it.
  • Dues tracking with automatic reminders. The site nudges late payers so your treasurer doesn't have to.
  • Raffles, carpools, van pools, lockers and a parade-ready checklist — the krewe-shaped things no membership platform does.
  • An engagement view so you can see who's drifting while there's still time to call them.
  • Officer reports — who's paid, who's ready, who's active, at a glance.

Why it's me

I've sat in the chair where these problems land.

A friend brought me to a parade and I fell in love with the happy people and the Gasparilla craziness. But I said yes to krewe life when I understood the fundraising and philanthropic side of it. The krewes of Tampa Bay support charities and groups that wouldn't have funding any other way. The beads and the costumes get photographed. The giving is the point.

I've served as Shamrock's social coordinator and run our raffle events, which means I've been the person chasing the headcount and the person running the raffle table. When I went looking for something that handled all of it, there wasn't one. So I built what our krewe actually needed, and then other krewes started asking.

Every hour a treasurer spends chasing dues on a spreadsheet is an hour not spent raising money for kids.

Nobody has to explain to me why parade-day headcount matters, or why dues season is miserable, or why the real job is making people feel like they're part of something. I believe in the Tampa Bay krewe world — not as a market, as a place I belong.

The questions every board asks

Fair ones. Here are the answers.

What happens if you disappear?

The right question to ask about anything custom. Three answers. Your krewe owns the code and the database outright — that's in the agreement, not a promise I make on a call. It's built on standard tools any developer can pick up, not something only I understand. And I document as I go, so you're not dependent on my memory.

If I got hit by a bus tomorrow, your krewe still has its site and its data.

Can our officers edit it themselves?

Yes. Officers get their own tools — post events, take RSVPs, mark dues paid, add photos, run a raffle — without touching code or calling me. Members maintain their own directory entries. My retainer covers the things you shouldn't have to do: hosting, backups, updates, seasonal changes and new features.

Isn't this just a template with our logo on it?

No — and the Inter-Krewe Council's own rules are why. Your name has to be unlike anyone else's and your costume has to be original. Handing you the same layout every other krewe gets would contradict the thing that makes you a krewe. Tell me your krewe's vision and I'll build you something nobody else has.

How long does it take, and what do you need from us?

Roughly six weeks for a full portal build, faster for the public site alone. What I need from you: your logo and photos, your history in whatever rough form it exists, your current member list, and your event calendar. I'll send a checklist so nobody has to guess.

If you want something live before Krewe Rush, work backwards from that date and start now.

We already pay for membership software. Why switch?

So did we, for years, and it handled a member list perfectly well. But it was built for chambers of commerce and alumni associations, so everything actually krewe-shaped still happened by hand — headcount in a group text, dues chased one member at a time, carpools in a thread nobody could find two days later.

I'm not trying to be cheaper than your platform this year. I'm trying to make sure your krewe isn't paying more every year for something it will never own.
